How long should you actually be in a calorie deficit?
This is where most people go wrong.
They pick 6, 8 or 12 weeks just because it sounds right, not because it's right for their body or goals.
And end up miserable the whole time wondering why they can't stick to it.
The truth, it's different for everyone.
It's as long as it takes for you to feel lean, toned and confident.
The more sustainable it feels, the faster you'll get to where you want to be.
So here's what you need to do.
Make your deficit something you can live with.
High protein, choose meals you actually enjoy and aim for balance instead of restriction.
And if you find yourself constantly struggling, you might need a diet break for 1 or 2 weekends.
To give yourself a short reset and get back on track.
And once you've reached your fat loss goal, don't keep starving yourself.
Slowly increase your calories, let your body adapt so you don't bounce back.
